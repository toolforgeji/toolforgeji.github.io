Understanding Crontab Syntax

01

5-Part Specification

Minute, Hour, Day-of-Month, Month, and Day-of-Week represent sequential time dimensions.

02

Wildcards & Steps

Asterisks * represent 'every', slashes / represent step intervals, and hyphens define ranges.

03

Client Simulation

Frequently Asked Questions

What cron format does this tool generate?
It generates standard POSIX crontab 5-field syntax: Minute (0-59), Hour (0-23), Day of Month (1-31), Month (1-12), and Day of Week (0-6).
Does Day 0 mean Sunday or Monday?
In standard crontab, 0 (and 7) represents Sunday, 1 is Monday, and 6 is Saturday.
Can I paste a cron expression into crontab -e?
Yes, simply copy the generated 5 fields followed by your command (e.g. 0 9 * * 1-5 /opt/scripts/backup.sh).
